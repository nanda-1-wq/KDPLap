## 1. KDP's rules for these trims (cited)

Source: KDP Help, "Set Trim Size, Bleed, and Margins" (kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/GVBQ3CMEQW3W2VL6, read Nov 17, 2026):
- Both are **large trim sizes** ("more than 6.12 in wide or more than 9 in high"), so printing costs more than for 6 × 9.
- **Page count limits (black ink):** 8 × 10: 24 to 828 pages on white paper, 24 to 776 on cream. 8.5 × 11: 24 to **590** on white, 24 to **550** on cream. Group A trims on cream: 24 to **776** (today we warn only above 828 on either paper).
- **Margins** use the same table for every trim. Inside: 0.375 in (24 to 150 pages), 0.5 in (151 to 300), 0.625 in (301 to 500), 0.75 in (501 to 700), 0.875 in (701 to 828). Top, bottom and outside: at least 0.25 in with no bleed, and 0.375 in with bleed.
- **Bleed:** none for our interior (text only, no picture reaches the edge). The PDF page is exactly the trim: 8 × 10 in and 8.5 × 11 in. With bleed it would be 8.125 × 10.25 and 8.625 × 11.25, which we do not use. The cover bleed (0.125 in) is already handled in 09.

Source: KDP Help, "Expanded Distribution" (topic GQTT4W3T5AYK7L45): 8 × 10 and 8.5 × 11 are eligible for Expanded Distribution with **white paper only** (cream: "No").

## 2. Typography for wide pages (the proposal)

Measured today (`tests/browser/format/out/engine-real.json`, 6 × 9, text width 4.75 in): characters per line are Classic 74, Modern 64, Clean 67, Warm 68.
With today's margins, an 8.5 × 11 page has a 7.25 in line. That is about **113 / 98 / 102 / 104 characters**, far past the 45 to 75 that reads comfortably.

**Recommended (A): a 5.5 in text width, type × 1.1, wide margins.**

| Trim | Inside | Outside | Top | Bottom | Text block |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 × 8 to 6 × 9 (unchanged) | 0.75 | 0.5 | 0.75 | 0.75 | as today |
| 8 × 10 | 1.125 | 1.375 | 0.875 | 0.875 | 5.5 × 8.25 in |
| 8.5 × 11 | 1.25 | 1.75 | 1.0 | 1.0 | 5.5 × 9.0 in |

- Type × 1.1 on large trims: Classic 13.2 pt, Modern 12.1, Clean 11.55, Warm 11.55. The fixed sizes scale too (header 8 → 8.8, footer, title page, copyright). Leading is in em, so it follows.
- Predicted characters per line: Classic 78, Modern 68, Clean 71, Warm 72. That is within about 6% of the same style at 6 × 9, so a style looks the same on every trim.
- Why the outside margin is wider than the inside one: on a spread, the two inside margins sit side by side. Inside 1.25 + 1.25, less what the binding hides, is about one outside margin. This is the classic page proportion, and it reads as a choice, not as empty space.
- Every margin is above KDP's minimum at every page count (inside 1.125 in or more against the highest tier, 0.875 in). The "Over 700 pages, KDP asks 0.875 in inside" warning therefore never shows on large trims.
- Why not B (today's margins with 11 to 12 pt type): 95 to 105 characters per line is hard to read for long nonfiction text.
- Why not two columns: the headings, lists, quotes and drop caps would all need new layout rules and a new design. That is out of scope.

The numbers are set in one place, `kdpFormatRules.layout(trim)` → `{ margin: { inside, outside, top, bottom }, scale }`. `md2typst` passes them to `book.typ`. `marginNote` and the Ready box read them from there too.

## 4. Page estimates (01 Brief, 05 Outline, 07, DOCX strip)

The page-area rule overstates large pages. Our bigger type and margins hold far fewer words than the page area suggests.
- Predicted words per page compared with 6 × 9: 8 × 10 about 1.05 ×, 8.5 × 11 about 1.15 × (today's rule says 1.48 × and 1.73 ×).
- `kdpWords.pages(words, trim)`: group A keeps the area rule exactly (no number on any existing book changes). The two large trims use a factor table, `LARGE_FACTOR = { '8x10': f1, '8.5x11': f2 }`. The values are the **measured** mean words-per-page ratio of the 4 styles from the real engine (Part 2).
- A test keeps them in step: the engine-measured ratio must equal the table within 5%.
- New `kdpWords.perPage(trim)`, used by the export dialog strip in place of its own copy of the formula.

## 8. Risks

- **R1 Group A drift:** threading margins through `geo` could move old books. Guarded by the byte-identical check.
- **R2 Estimate drift:** the large-trim factors come from a placeholder book. A real book with many lists or headings differs. The 10% spread and the 5% factor test catch large gaps. The estimate stays a guess ("About N pages").
- **R3 Old briefs at 8.5x11:** these books (the old "extra" value) see new page estimates and can now make a Print PDF. No data changes. Their estimates drop compared with today's area rule.
- **R4 Words per page spread over 10%** at a new trim. That would mean the × 1.1 scale must differ by style. Part 1 shows it before any code.
- **R5 Page caps:** 8.5 × 11 caps at 590 / 550 pages. 09's `PAGES_MAX` stays 828 (E16 is not changed here). 08 and 07 warn first.
- **R6 KDP Previewer** could flag something new at large trims (for example the outside margin or the header position). STOP 2 finds this before any screen work.
- **R7 Cost:** large trims print at a higher cost (KDP). Nothing in the app shows the price today, so this needs no change. It is noted for the roadmap.
